\label{chap:conclusions}

This study successfully designed, implemented, and rigorously evaluated the \textbf{TransferKAN} architecture for the automated classification of human gunshot wounds. By merging the powerful spatial feature extraction capabilities of a pre-trained ResNet152 backbone with the dynamic, parameter-efficient, non-linear modeling of a Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN), we offer a reference evaluated under a deliberately harsh protocol for deep learning applications in forensic pathology.

The empirical analysis, conducted on a realistic and severely imbalanced dataset of 2,551 crime scene images (FDCPUnBGunshotDB), demonstrated that the TransferKAN model significantly improves classification robustness and generalization over traditional methods. Specifically, in the binary task of distinguishing between Entry and Exit wounds, the proposed architecture achieved a stellar micro Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.928 and a macro AUC of 0.908 under a strict case-based 5-fold cross-validation scheme. This performance outperforms the baseline ResNet152 classifier reported in the 2024 literature~\cite{Lira2025deep} (which achieved a uniform AUC of 0.821), supporting the claim that KANs offer an advantage in probabilistically handling minority classes and non-linear feature boundaries without collapsing onto the majority class.

For the Medical-Legal Shooting Distance (MLSD) classification, despite the inherent difficulties of extreme class imbalance and the stringent cross-validation protocol actively preventing data leakage, the TransferKAN model yielded a higher Macro AUC (0.749) and Weighted AUC (0.785) compared to the traditional CNN baselines. These results support our central hypothesis: replacing the standard, rigid Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with a flexible Kolmogorov-Arnold Network enhances the model's capacity to discriminate complex visual patterns in noisy forensic images.

In conclusion, our approach utilizing TransferKAN with strict, case-isolated k-fold validation subjects the model to a more demanding evaluation protocol than is customary in this domain, and still delivers an AUC gain on both tasks---consistent evidence of its viability as a supportive tool for forensic experts and medical examiners. The findings should nonetheless be read in light of the threats to validity recorded in Section~\ref{sec:threats}: the selection of the checkpoint on each fold's own evaluation set, the non-isometric nature of the comparison with the ablation architectures, and the bibliographic---rather than experimental---provenance of the baseline metrics.

Future work should focus, as a priority, on two methodological corrections that would make the comparison definitive: re-running the baseline ResNet152 locally under exactly the same case-isolated 5-fold cross-validation scheme, eliminating the protocol asymmetry that currently separates the two models; and adopting, within each fold, an inner validation partition---likewise separated by case---dedicated to epoch selection, so as to remove the optimistic bias from the threshold-dependent metrics. It would be equally valuable to re-evaluate the ablation architectures at the same resolution, batch size, and protocol as TransferKAN, turning the comparative study into a controlled ablation. Beyond that, expanding the dataset through multi-institutional collaborations would allow the model to be validated across diverse demographics and firearm types. Additionally, an exciting avenue for future research involves exploring the mathematical interpretability of the KAN splines. Because the activation functions are explicit piecewise polynomials on the edges, they offer a unique opportunity to develop Explainable AI (XAI) insights, potentially allowing forensic pathologists to physically interpret which visual features the network prioritizes during its decision-making process. Finally, investigating the integration of multimodal data, such as incorporating the raw text of autopsy reports alongside the digital images, could further refine the classification pipeline.
