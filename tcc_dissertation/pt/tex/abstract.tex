Classifying gunshot wounds as entry or exit and estimating the shooting distance are demanding forensic tasks. This work evaluates TransferKAN, a ResNet-152 pre-trained on ImageNet with a Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) as its classification head, on real crime-scene photographs of the FDCPUnBGunshotDB. An earlier version of this work reported that TransferKAN outperformed the baselines of Lira et al., but that comparison mixed different splits, initialisations and definitions of the AUC. The evaluation was therefore rebuilt under a single protocol: five folds grouped by case, the same training recipe for every model, AUC from probabilities and decision rules fixed before the experiments.

Four findings follow. (i) The hold-out of the public code of the 2024 study stratifies by case instead of grouping by case: 98.7\% of its entry/exit test images belong to cases seen in training. With the same network and recipe, it inflates the AUC by $+0.034$ in entry/exit and by $+0.155$ to $+0.158$ in shooting distance. (ii) The KAN head is not the source of the gains: it is equivalent to linear and MLP heads in entry/exit (AUC 0.905 against 0.906) and below an MLP of the same size in shooting distance, and a wavelet KAN does not change this. (iii) Among twelve retrained architectures, Swin-B is the best (AUC 0.929 in entry/exit) and outperforms TransferKAN in both tasks. (iv) A single network with one head per task has 47--50\% fewer parameters and is twice as fast as the pair of networks. It is non-inferior in shooting distance and about 0.01 of AUC worse in entry/exit, within the pre-registered margin. Contact shots remain the main limitation. The contributions are an audited split, a leak-free protocol and a multitask network at half the cost.
